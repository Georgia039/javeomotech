What is the EDC Pry Bar Multitool?

I'll be honest — when I first heard "pry bar multitool," I thought it was going to be one of those gadgets that does ten things and none of them well. I've been burned before. But I kept seeing this thing come up and figured, why not. I already carry a pocket knife daily and half the time I'm using the blade to pry open a box or pop a bottle cap, which, yeah, I know — not great for the blade. So the idea of having a dedicated flat bar for all that stuff actually made sense to me. This is one of those tools that sounds gimmicky until you actually carry it for a week. It's a flat, pocket-sized pry bar that packs in a bi-directional ratchet screwdriver, multiple wrench sizes, a bottle opener, a box cutter edge, and even a built-in pen — all in something thin enough to clip to your belt or slide into a pocket without noticing it's there.

Out of the box it's thinner than I expected, which is a good thing. It clips onto your pocket or belt and genuinely disappears. I forgot it was on me within about an hour, which for a pocket tool is pretty much the highest compliment you can give.

What It's Actually Like to Use

The ratchet screwdriver is where things get interesting. Bi-directional ratcheting means you don't have to lift and reset after every turn, which sounds like a small thing until you've spent five minutes tightening a screw with a regular flathead. In practice though, you do need to keep a firm grip — the bit isn't locked in magnetically or mechanically, so if you angle it wrong it'll slide out mid-turn. Annoying? A little. Deal-breaking? Not really, you just adapt.

The ratchet screwdriver is the standout feature. Instead of pulling the bit out and resetting direction every turn like a basic flathead, it ratchets both ways, which makes actual repetitive screwing way less annoying. The wrench cutouts are sized for common bolts, so it covers a decent range without needing a full wrench set.

Bottle opener works exactly as expected. Opened a few bottles, zero issues. The built-in pen is more of a "nice to have in a pinch" situation — don't expect it to replace your actual pen, but it's there when you need to scribble something and have nothing else on you.

If you're the person who's always asked "does anyone have a screwdriver?" at work, or you just want one solid tool to throw in your car, bag, or pocket for daily annoyances, this is an easy yes. At under $35, it earns its spot fast.
