📝 Full Review

I'll be honest — when I first heard "pry bar multitool," I thought it was going to be one of those gadgets that does ten things and none of them well. I've been burned before. But I kept seeing this thing come up and figured, why not. I already carry a pocket knife daily and half the time I'm using the blade to pry open a box or pop a bottle cap, which, yeah, I know — not great for the blade. So the idea of having a dedicated flat bar for all that stuff actually made sense to me.

This is one of those tools that sounds gimmicky until you actually carry it for a week. It's a flat, pocket-sized pry bar that packs in a bi-directional ratchet screwdriver, multiple wrench sizes, a bottle opener, a box cutter edge, and even a built-in pen — all in something thin enough to clip to your belt or slide into a pocket without noticing it's there.

In hand — sits flat against the palm, nothing digs in.

Just How Small Is It?

Numbers on a spec sheet don't always tell you much, so I laid it next to a regular pencil and then a ruler to get an actual sense of scale. It's genuinely pocket-sized — closer to the length of a standard pen than anything you'd think of as a "tool."

*Next to a pencil, and against a ruler — genuinely pocket-sized.*

What It's Actually Like to Use

The ratchet screwdriver is where things get interesting. Bi-directional ratcheting means you don't have to lift and reset after every turn, which sounds like a small thing until you've spent five minutes tightening a screw with a regular flathead. In practice though, you do need to keep a firm grip — the bit isn't locked in magnetically or mechanically, so if you angle it wrong it'll slide out mid-turn. Annoying? A little. Deal-breaking? Not really, you just adapt.

Close-up on the ratchet screwdriver bit.

⚙️

Smart Bi-Directional Design

The ratcheting mechanism streamlines driving fasteners without requiring constant repositioning of your hand or wrist.

The wrench cutouts are sized for common bolts, so it covers a decent range without needing a full wrench set. The bottle opener works exactly as expected, and the built-in pen serves nicely as a backup writer when you're caught without your regular gear.

Out of the box, nothing feels overly bulky. It clips onto your pocket or belt and genuinely disappears. I forgot it was on me within about an hour, which for a pocket tool is pretty much the highest compliment you can give.

In the Box

Nothing fancy here — the bar itself, the clip, and a small instruction booklet walking through each of the eleven functions. Worth actually skimming once, since a couple of the wrench cutouts and the ratchet direction switch aren't obvious just from looking at the tool.

First page of the included instruction booklet.

Who Should Buy This?

If you're the person who's always asked "does anyone have a screwdriver?" at work, or you just want one solid tool to throw in your car, bag, or pocket for daily annoyances, this is an easy yes. At under $35, it earns its spot fast.

If you need precision torque or are doing actual mechanical work, this is a backup tool, not your main one. But for opening boxes, tightening loose screws, and popping bottles on the go, it's hard to beat.
