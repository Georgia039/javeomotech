What Is This Thing?

This is one of those "solve three problems with one cable" products — a retractable cord that ends in a little connector hub with Lightning, USB-C, and Micro USB tips built in. The idea is simple: instead of keeping three separate cables around for your phone, your partner's phone, your tablet, and whatever random Micro USB device still exists in your house, you carry one cable that covers all of it. At $11.04 for a two-pack, the price makes it an easy thing to just try.

Two cables in the box — enough to keep one in the car and one at your desk.

The Retractable Part Actually Works Well

I was a little skeptical of the retraction mechanism going in — a lot of retractable cables I've tried in the past get stiff or stop retracting evenly after a few weeks. This one has held up fine so far. You pull out however much length you need, and it reels back in cleanly without getting stuck halfway. For a bag, a car console, or a desk where you don't want three feet of loose cable flopping around, this genuinely solves the tangle problem. It's a small thing, but it's the kind of small thing you notice every single day you use it.

Compact enough to hold in one hand, tips included, easy to stash anywhere.

Charging Speed — Fine for Most, Not a Speed Demon

Charging speed here is a mixed bag depending on who you ask, and I get why. On my phone, it charges steadily and doesn't run hot, which I actually care about more than raw speed — a cable that gets warm during a long charge is a cable I don't trust long term. That said, don't expect blazing fast charging. If you're used to a proper high-wattage brick and cable combo, this will feel noticeably slower by comparison. For topping up during the day or overnight charging, it's completely fine. For "I need 50% in ten minutes before I run out the door," it's not the cable for that.

The Three Tips — What They're Actually Good For

Having all three connector types in one place is honestly the best part of owning this. If you've got an iPhone person and an Android person in the same house — or you're juggling a tablet, a PS controller, and old wired earbuds that all use different ports — this cable ends the "whose charger is this" argument. One heads-up worth being upfront about, though: a couple of people have found that these are charge-only through certain ports on certain phones — meaning if you're trying to use it for data transfer or something like Android Auto in your car, it may not pick up the phone for that. If you specifically need a cable that both charges and handles a wired Android Auto or CarPlay connection, don't rely on this being that cable — test it with your exact phone and car setup before committing to it as your only option there.

All three tips in one — Lightning, USB-C, and Micro USB, covering most of what you'll actually own.

The Honest Part: Longevity

Here's where I have to be straight with you. This isn't a cable I'd expect to survive years of daily wear. It's genuinely great for the first several months — reliable charging, clean retraction, no fraying. But cables at this price point in this category tend to develop the classic symptoms over time: needing to be positioned just right to make contact, or only charging when angled a certain way. Whether that happens to you at six months or a year probably depends on how roughly it gets handled — tossed in a bag daily versus living calmly on a desk are very different lifespans. I wouldn't call this a forever-cable. I'd call it a really convenient, cheap-enough-to-replace cable that does its job well while it lasts.

Is that a dealbreaker? For me, not really — at this price, treating it as a low-commitment convenience item rather than a lifetime purchase feels like the right way to think about it. Buy it knowing you might be back for a replacement down the line, and you won't feel let down when that day comes.

Who Should Buy This?

If your household has a mix of device types and you're tired of hunting for the right cable, or you just want one clean retractable cord for your bag, car, or desk without buying three separate cables, this is a solid, cheap pickup. If you need something built to survive years of heavy daily use without any degradation, or you're relying on it for data transfer through your car's system, temper your expectations or test it first. For casual, everyday charging convenience at under $12 for two, it earns its spot in the drawer.
