📝 Full Review

I'll be honest — I didn't expect much from a $19.99 pair of AI sunglasses. I've tried enough cheap smart wearables to be skeptical of anything promising DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.5, 164-language translation, photochromic lenses, and IP67 water resistance all at once. But after actually wearing these day to day, I've come away genuinely impressed. Are they perfect? No. But for the price, they're one of the more surprising gadgets I've reviewed.

Sleek temple design housing open-ear directional speakers and dual noise-canceling mics.

How They Feel Day to Day

The first thing I noticed pulling these out of the box was how light they are. I was bracing for that front-heavy feeling a lot of smart glasses have after an hour of wear, but these sit on my face like a regular pair of sunglasses. I've worn them through full work days and long walks without any pinch behind the ears or bridge fatigue — honestly, I forget I'm wearing them half the time.

The IP67 rating has come in handy more than I expected. I've gotten caught in rain without thinking twice about it, and they've held up fine through sweaty outdoor sessions too. For something this cheap, that kind of build confidence is a pleasant surprise.

The Lenses: Photochromic, But Not Instant

The blue-light-to-sunglasses transition is a feature I actually use — indoors they sit clear for screen time, and outside they darken automatically. Do I love it? Mostly. My one honest gripe is that the transition isn't instant. Step outside into bright sun and there's a noticeable lag before they fully darken. If you're expecting them to snap to full tint the second you're outdoors, they won't — think gradual transition lenses, not proper polarized aviators.

💡

My Honest Score — 8.6/10

For $19.99, I don't think there's much else on the market that matches what these deliver. They're not flawless, but the value here is real — I'd recommend them to anyone curious about AI wearables without wanting to spend $300+.

Audio & Calls — This Is What I Actually Use Most

If I'm honest, the speakers and hands-free calling are the feature I reach for the most, by far. The open-ear audio isn't going to replace real headphones — there's no deep bass — but for podcasts, calls, and casual music while I'm moving around, it's clear and plenty loud. The dual mics do a solid job cutting background noise on calls too, so the person on the other end isn't fighting wind or street noise to hear me.

The privacy mode for calls is a nice touch — it directs sound into your ear rather than blasting it out, so I don't feel like I'm broadcasting my conversations to everyone nearby.

Top-down view detailing the slim profile and minimal bulk of the battery-housing temples.

The AI and Translation Features

The app pairs up with DeepSeek-R1 and Gemini-2.5, and honestly it's more capable than I expected for the price — I've used the touch control to ask questions, get quick summaries, and get walking directions without pulling out my phone. The 164-language translation is a neat trick too, though I'll admit it's not something I reach for daily. It's the kind of feature I'm glad exists for travel, even if music and calls are what I actually use most.

The Honest Con: Battery Life

Here's my one real complaint — battery life is shorter than I expected going in. If you're using the speakers and AI features regularly, don't expect to go multiple full days without a charge. It's not a dealbreaker for me since I charge them alongside my other devices, but if you want all-day-every-day wear without thinking about a charger, this is the tradeoff to know about upfront.

My Final Take

Overall, I'm genuinely impressed by what these deliver for $19.99. The speakers and hands-free calling get the most use out of me day to day, the photochromic lenses and AI features are a legitimately useful bonus, and the battery life is really the only thing holding me back from a higher score. If you're looking for an affordable way into AI wearables, I think these are worth it.
