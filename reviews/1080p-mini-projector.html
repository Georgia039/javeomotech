📝 Full Review

First Impressions

I'll be upfront — I wasn't expecting much from a projector under $70. Most things in this price range make big claims on the box and disappoint you within the first hour. This one surprised me. Setup from unboxing to watching something took maybe ten minutes, and the electric focus got me a sharp image on my bedroom wall without any real effort. That alone set the tone.

Clean unboxing — projector, remote, power cable, and a basic manual.

🎬

Built-in Netflix Is the Real Selling Point

Not having to plug in a Chromecast or Fire Stick every time is genuinely more convenient than it sounds. You turn it on, open Netflix, and watch. That's it. For bedroom movie nights this changes the whole experience — less cable clutter, less setup friction.

Compact side profile — about the size of a large water bottle, lighter than it looks.

Picture Quality — Better Than Expected, With Caveats

In a properly dark room, the image is genuinely good. Colors are vibrant, the 1080P sharpness is real, and the electric focus gets you a crisp image fast. I tested it projecting a river and mountain landscape and the detail was solid — nothing that'll rival a £1,000 projector, but for under $70 it's impressive.

River and mountain landscape projected onto the wall — colors are punchy and detailed in a dark room.

Blues and fine detail hold up well — this is the kind of content it handles best.

The honest caveat is brightness. This is not a projector you can use in a room with the lights on or curtains open. Ambient light kills the picture noticeably. Think of it as a dedicated dark-room projector rather than a general-purpose TV replacement. For bedroom use at night it's excellent. For a living room in the afternoon it'll frustrate you.

⚠️

Not Built for Bright Rooms or Outdoors

Multiple reviewers have flagged this, and my experience matches — in anything other than a properly dark room, the image washes out significantly. If you're planning to use this camping or in a lit room, manage your expectations or look at a higher-lumen option.

Netflix & Built-in Apps

The built-in app experience is one of the genuine highlights here. Netflix loads cleanly, the interface is smooth, and the WiFi connection stayed stable throughout my testing. YouTube works well too. The only friction point is text input — if you need to search for something, you're using arrow keys on the remote to click through an on-screen keyboard letter by letter. There's a voice search icon on the interface but it's essentially decorative. A Bluetooth keyboard would solve this completely if it bothers you.

Netflix running natively — no streaming stick required, straight out of the box.

Connectivity — Read This Before You Buy

This is where the projector's limitations are most obvious, and it's worth being clear about them. HDMI works reliably and is the best way to connect an external device. Wireless casting is a different story — several reviewers noted that AirPlay didn't work despite being listed as compatible, and getting cast quality above 480p wirelessly was inconsistent. Bluetooth is audio output only, not a screen connection method. USB is for drives, not display input.

If you're planning to use this as a wireless second monitor for a PC, it won't work the way you might expect. If you're planning to connect a laptop or console via HDMI for movies, it works fine. Know your use case before buying.

Audio & Build Quality

The built-in speaker is passable for casual watching — fine for a bedroom where you're close to the projector, thin if you want proper movie audio. Pairing a Bluetooth speaker is straightforward and makes a noticeable difference. The unit runs warm during extended use, and there's a faint plastic smell for the first hour or so that fades after a few sessions. The remote runs on two AAA batteries (not included — worth noting).

Who Is This For?

Someone who wants a dedicated bedroom movie projector, uses it at night in a dark room, and values the convenience of built-in Netflix over raw performance. It's also a solid pick for parents setting up a casual movie setup for kids — several reviewers mentioned exactly this use case and it fits perfectly. If you want to game on it, use it outdoors, or replace a TV in a lit living room, look elsewhere. But for what it's designed for — affordable, convenient home movie nights — it delivers.
