📝 Full Review

Let me be honest — I almost glossed over this one. An electric screwdriver at $70 sounds like a lot for something that's basically a powered version of what you can do by hand. But after using the Fanttik S2 Pro on an actual IKEA build, I get it now.

Design and Build Quality

The first thing that stands out is how solid it feels the moment you pick it up. It's got some actual heft — not heavy, but dense in a way that reads as quality. The bits click into place with a satisfying snap, the grip is comfortable, and the button layout makes sense. Nothing about it feels cheap, which you don't always get at this price point.

All 20 bits sit neatly in the case — nothing rolling around.

Real-World Performance and Torque Dial

For furniture assembly, this thing really does shine. I used it on a bedframe with a mix of hex bolts and Phillips screws and it handled everything cleanly without stripping a single head. The torque dial is genuinely useful too — not just a marketing spec. Dropping it to the lower settings for electronics or outlet covers, then cranking it up for MDF, makes a real difference.

Everything in the box, organized and ready to go.

The 90° Adapter Difference

The 90° angle adapter is where it gets interesting. Anyone who's tried to drive screws into a tight corner with a full-size drill knows the frustration. The L-adapter on the S2 Pro solves that without drama. It had enough torque to drive into drywall anchors at an angle — something I honestly didn't expect.

The L-adapter getting into a corner a full-size drill can't reach.

Limitations and Final Verdict

Am I being too optimistic? Maybe. It's not a replacement for a real cordless drill. If you're driving long screws into hardwood or building a deck, go get something with actual voltage. But for everything else — furniture, household repairs, small electronics, anything where precision matters more than brute force — this is the right tool and the full-size driver is the wrong one.

The trigger is the one thing that bugs me slightly. It feels a touch mushy compared to the rest of the build quality, which makes variable speed control a bit less precise than I'd like. It's not a dealbreaker, not even close, but it's the one place where the S2 Pro feels like it's a small step below its own standard. The case could also be a bit more durable — it's fine for storage and charging, but I wouldn't drop it.

Bottom line: if you're tired of reaching for your big drill for every little household task and stripping screws in the process, the Fanttik S2 Pro is the smarter option. Compact, organised, well-made — trust me on this one.
