Full Review

What Is the AirFly Pro 2?

I don't usually get this excited about accessories. But the AirFly Pro 2 genuinely surprised me — and I say that as someone who's been through enough travel gadgets to know when something is actually solving a problem versus just adding complexity. This is a Bluetooth transmitter the size of a lipstick cap that plugs into any 3.5mm headphone jack and turns it wireless.

Plug it into the seat jack, pair your AirPods, done. Setup takes about 10 seconds.

On a 12-Hour Flight

I tested this on a 12-hour Delta flight and it made the entire experience genuinely better. Plugged into the headphone jack on the in-flight entertainment screen, pressed the button, my Bose QuietComfort headphones connected within seconds. Audio came through crystal clear — no dropouts, no weird delay, no sync issues during movies. I watched a few films back to back and never once had to fiddle with it. It just worked.

The audio quality is excellent. I was skeptical going in — Bluetooth over a 3.5mm transmitter sounds like it should be a compromise, but it doesn't feel like one in practice. The QuietComfort headphones sound like they always do, which is all you can really ask for.

Plugged directly into the seat entertainment jack — no adapters, no fuss.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

The Family Travel Game-Changer

Connect the AirFly Pro 2 to a USB-C headphone adapter on your iPad, and multiple kids can pair their own wireless headphones to the same iPad simultaneously. No more sharing one pair of earbuds or fighting over the volume. Tested this on road trips — it works exactly as described.

Dual Headphone Pairing

This is the feature that pushed me from "good gadget" to "genuinely recommend to everyone." The AirFly Pro 2 supports two Bluetooth headphone connections at the same time. On the flight, my travel companion and I both connected our own headphones to the same seat entertainment jack and watched the same movie together, completely wire-free. That's a feature I didn't know I wanted until I had it, and now I can't imagine flying without it.

Beyond the Airplane

I've been reaching for this thing in situations I didn't originally buy it for. Hotel TVs with no Bluetooth. The treadmill at the gym. My car's aux port when I want to use headphones instead of speakers. The PS5 controller. Anything with a 3.5mm jack becomes wireless-headphone compatible — it quietly removes a lot of friction from daily life once you start thinking of it that way.

Build quality is solid — it's been in my bag on multiple trips and still looks new.

What It's Missing

A few genuine gaps worth knowing about. There's no LCD screen, so you can't see which headphones are connected or what the battery percentage is. The Avantree Voyager has this and it's a legitimate advantage if you're managing multiple pairs. The AirFly only comes in white, which won't match most airplane seat jacks. It doesn't come with a hard case — just a soft pouch. And the connection process, while generally smooth, isn't completely obvious the first time. Read the instructions before your first flight, not at 35,000 feet.

None of these are dealbreakers. The core experience — wireless audio from any 3.5mm source, zero lag, dual pairing — is executed so well that the missing features feel like future wishlist items rather than reasons not to buy.

Who Should Buy This?

Anyone who travels regularly and uses wireless headphones. Full stop. If you're on planes more than twice a year, this pays for itself in comfort before the end of your second flight. At $59.99 it's not cheap for an adapter, but it's the kind of thing that quietly becomes a permanent resident in your travel bag. Trust me on this one.
